# lucene-codecs (M14.g)

[Index](../parity.md). `org.apache.lucene.codecs.*` in the `lucene-codecs` jar: the non-default
postings formats and codecs. The default codec's formats are in [codecs.md](codecs.md); the
class-by-class triage is `docs/inventory/lucene-codecs.tsv`. These formats are module-level readers
and writers over a group's files, like sandbox's `IDVersion` ([sandbox.md](sandbox.md)): this
port's `IndexWriter` routes no field to them and `DirectoryReader` does not open them. Unit tests
without a Java counterpart: `lucene-codecs/src/bloom/tests.rs`.

| Java | Rust | Status |
|---|---|---|
| `index/{SegmentWriteState, SegmentReadState}` | `lucene-codecs/src/segment_state.rs::{SegmentWriteState, SegmentReadState}` | **ported** M14.g: what a wrapping postings format is constructed with -- `maxDoc`, the segment id, the codec suffix, the whole segment's `FieldInfos` (and from it `hasProx`/`hasPayloads || hasOffsets`, which decide `Lucene104PostingsWriter`'s `.pos`/`.pay`); the carrier for every M14.g postings format. Differs: no `Directory`, segment name or `IOContext` -- files go in and come out as bytes; the default codec's writers still take the id and suffix as arguments. Tests: its own unit test, and every bloom fixture. |
| `codecs/bloom/{BloomFilteringPostingsFormat, BloomFilterFactory, DefaultBloomFilterFactory}` | `lucene-codecs/src/bloom/mod.rs::{BloomFilteringPostingsFormat, BloomFilterFactory, DefaultBloomFilterFactory, BloomFilteredFieldsProducer, BloomFilteredTerms, BloomOutput, check_blm_footer}` | **ported** M14.g: `.blm` (header `BloomFilter` v3, delegate name, each kept filter's field number and `FuzzySet`, footer); every term added to `getSetForField`'s set, saturated sets dropped, the rest downsized when the factory allows; the reader opens the delegate under the same suffix, `seekExact` answered by the filter's `NO`. Byte-identical `.blm`s rebuilt from a flushed (deletes) and a force-merged segment's terms, two groups (default and 4/9 delegates, one filter saturated), and `contains`, `seekExact`, `docFreq` equal over every 13th present term and 400 absent ones -- `GenCodecsBloom` -> `codecs_bloom_fixtures.rs`; Lucene reads Rust-written groups back (each filter's `toString`, `MAYBE` for every written term, this port's `MAYBE`/`NO` for 3,000 absent terms a field, every term walked and sought) -- `write_bloom_fixture` -> `VerifyCodecsBloom`. Java's `.blm`s hold one entry each: a multi-entry one is proven only by Lucene reading Rust's. Differs: entries in field-number order (Java: `HashMap<FieldInfo>` identity order, which varies by run); the delegate must be a `Lucene104PostingsFormat` (the only one this port writes) and, read, one `PostingsFormat::from_name` knows -- only `Lucene104` is exercised; `check_blm_footer` checks the `.blm` alone (Java's `checkIntegrity` checks the delegate, the `.blm` at open). Gap: only `try_seek_exact` goes through the filter -- `iter()`, `intersect`, `seekCeil` and `ord` are the delegate's own, and `prepareSeekExact`/`preferSeekExact` and the `has*` flags are not exposed. Bench (`bench-micro.sh codecs`, 200,000 lookups, a quarter present): `cx_bloom_seek` 1.09x; the write side (one `add_value` a term) is not benchmarked. |
| `codecs/bloom/FuzzySet` | `lucene-codecs/src/bloom/fuzzy_set.rs::{FuzzySet, ContainsResult, get_nearest_set_size, get_nearest_set_size_for_saturation, get_estimated_number_unique_values_allowing_for_collisions}` | **ported** M14.g: the power-of-two sizes, `createSetBasedOnMaxMemory`/`createSetBasedOnQuality`/`createOptimalSet` (Java's `Math.log`/`pow` through `strict_math`, `float` rounding), `murmurhash3_x64_128` (seed 104729) positions, `downsize` folding to the smallest size under the target saturation, the estimated unique count, `serialize`/`deserialize` with Java's checks. Equal to Lucene's over 49 sizing and estimate rows and 18 random sets' `toString`, saturation, downsizing and serialized bytes, and three max-memory sets' `contains` over 200 probes -- `codecs_bloom_fixtures.rs`. Differs: `deserialize` reads the bits in 512-long chunks (a corrupt length fails at the end of input rather than allocating it), refuses a negative or `Integer.MAX_VALUE` `bloomSize` (Java accepts them), drops surplus longs and clears bits past `bloomSize` (Java keeps them, in its cardinality); Java uses `Math.log`/`pow`, which a JIT may compute other than `StrictMath` -- the default factory's constants agree either way. |
